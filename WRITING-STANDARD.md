# The Med Frontier Writing Standard

Use this checklist for every new page and every article revision.

## Voice

- Write like Jack: a high school student who reads the research because he is genuinely curious, then tells readers what he thinks.
- Prefer plain words and direct claims. Sound interested, skeptical, or surprised when the evidence earns that reaction.
- Use first person when a judgment is Jack's. Do not hide opinions behind institutional or press-release language.
- Keep concrete details: study names, patient counts, doses, percentages, time periods, and mechanisms.
- Vary sentence length. A short sentence is useful. So is an occasional fragment.
- Let paragraphs move by logic or curiosity, not stock transitions.

## Cut or rewrite

- Stock LLM words: delve, moreover, furthermore, underscore, boasts, robust, landscape, realm, tapestry, navigate, leverage, utilize.
- Stock throat-clearing: “in today's world,” “it's important to note,” “it's worth noting,” “at the end of the day,” “in conclusion,” and “in summary.”
- Automatic three-part lists when two items or four uneven examples would sound more honest.
- Reflexive “on one hand/on the other hand” balance and “while X has benefits, it also has drawbacks.”
- Sentences that repeat the previous paragraph or conclusions that simply replay the introduction.
- Generic transitions such as “Additionally,” “Furthermore,” “That said,” and “With that in mind.”
- Empty importance labels such as crucial, significant, essential, groundbreaking, transformative, or game-changing. Show the consequence instead.
- Corporate optimism, vague praise, and claims that never name the evidence.
- Repeated em dashes, semicolons, and identical sentence shapes.
- Template titles such as “The Future of X,” “What You Need to Know,” “A Deep Dive,” and reflexive “X: Is Y?” constructions.
- “X is this, not that” slogans. State the actual point.

## Final pass

1. Read the title, deck, headings, body, captions, card description, search text, and bio aloud.
2. Confirm every factual claim against the linked source.
3. Search the whole site for the banned words and stock phrases above.
4. Look for three-item lists, repeated paragraph endings, and evenly paced sentences by eye.
5. Keep a normal article near 750–1,000 words unless the subject clearly deserves a longer treatment.
6. Check the article image at desktop and mobile sizes. Article heroes must request a full-width source image and use `object-fit: cover` or `background-size: cover` without stretching.

## Why this standard exists

Research on LLM-assisted writing finds overrepresented vocabulary and less stylistic variation in machine-generated prose. Research on detectors also shows that detector scores are unstable across writing styles, so the target is not to “beat” a detector. The target is writing that is specific, candid, varied, and recognizably Jack's.
