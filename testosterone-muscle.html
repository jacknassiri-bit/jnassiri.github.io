Testosterone and Muscle Growth: What Is Real and What Gets Exaggerated

July 20, 2026·7 min read

Quick takeaways

  • Testosterone does affect muscle, and that is the reason its risks deserve attention.
  • Nonmedical use is different from medically supervised treatment for low testosterone.
  • Side effects can include fertility suppression, acne, lipid changes, mood changes, and cardiovascular concerns.
  • For teenagers, hormone experimentation is a bad trade for gym progress.

Testosterone can increase size and strength

Testosterone affects muscle size and strength along with red blood cell production, libido and recovery. Its effects on muscle are well established. The decisions people make from that fact deserve more attention.

A classic Bhasin et al. trial assigned 43 healthy men to placebo or testosterone, with or without strength training. The testosterone groups gained more fat-free mass, and testosterone combined with training produced the largest strength and size changes (Bhasin et al.). Results that clear are why the risks need equal attention.

Therapy and gym experimenting are very different

Testosterone therapy for diagnosed hypogonadism involves symptoms, laboratory testing, follow-up, dose adjustment and monitoring. Someone buying hormones for faster gym results has none of that medical structure.

The Endocrine Society guideline covers diagnosing hypogonadism, checking symptoms and lab values, and monitoring treatment over time (Bhasin et al.). Ordering hormones online because progress feels slow has none of that.

The before-and-after photos online rarely show what happens after stopping. Natural production may stay suppressed for a while, and some changes are not easy to reverse. A short cycle can create a much longer medical problem.

What gets left out online

Online posts tend to show the physique and skip the biology. Testosterone can suppress natural production, reduce fertility, worsen acne, affect hair loss risk, shift cholesterol markers, and change mood. Some risks depend on dose, duration, genetics, and whether other drugs are stacked with it.

For a teenager, the tradeoff is even worse. The endocrine system is still developing, and using hormones for gym progress can create problems that are much bigger than a plateau on bench press. Pope et al. reviewed performance-enhancing drug harms and described endocrine, psychiatric, cardiovascular, and liver-related risks across these drugs (Pope et al.). Outcomes vary between users, and the possible harm goes well beyond acne and mood swings.

My Thoughts

Testosterone's effect on muscle also shows why casual use can affect much more than a workout.

If the goal is building muscle without a medical need, I would stay with progressive training and enough food. Hormones belong in a clinic. A group-chat protocol cannot monitor blood pressure, fertility or what is actually in the vial.

I understand the attraction because strength and size arrive on a shorter timeline than cardiovascular or fertility problems. I would weigh both timelines before making the decision.
